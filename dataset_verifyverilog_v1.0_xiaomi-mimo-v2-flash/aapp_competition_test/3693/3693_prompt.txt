You are to design a Verilog module that determines if two squares intersect. One square is axis-aligned, the other is rotated by 45 degrees.

**Input Constraints:**
- Coordinates are integers between -100 and 100.
- We will scale coordinates by adding 100 to map them to [0, 200].
- The axis-aligned square is defined by 4 vertices (x1, y1), (x2, y2), (x3, y3), (x4, y4). Since it's axis-aligned, the min and max x and y define the bounding box.
- The rotated square is defined by 4 vertices. In the rotated frame (u, v) where u = x + y, v = x - y (scaled appropriately), the square is axis-aligned.

**Algorithm:**
1.  **Preprocessing (Combinational):**
    *   Extract bounding box for Axis-Aligned Square (A):
        *   `a_x_min` = min(x1, x2, x3, x4)
        *   `a_x_max` = max(x1, x2, x3, x4)
        *   `a_y_min` = min(y1, y2, y3, y4)
        *   `a_y_max` = max(y1, y2, y3, y4)
    *   Extract bounding box for Rotated Square (B) in (u, v) space:
        *   Transform all 4 vertices of B: `u_i = x_i + y_i`, `v_i = x_i - y_i` (Use scaled values: `x_scaled = x_orig + 100`, `y_scaled = y_orig + 100`). Range of `u` is [0, 400], `v` is [-200, 200].
        *   `b_u_min` = min(u_i)
        *   `b_u_max` = max(u_i)
        *   `b_v_min` = min(v_i)
        *   `b_v_max` = max(v_i)

2.  **Intersection Check (Sequential/Iterative):**
    *   Since we cannot iterate over the full 200x200 grid in reasonable cycles for all problems, we will limit the check to a representative sampling or bounding box logic. However, for high accuracy in HDL, a fixed number of sample points from the axis-aligned square is acceptable (e.g., 16x16 grid or 256 points).
    *   **Constraint:** We will iterate through `x` from `a_x_min` to `a_x_max` in steps, and `y` from `a_y_min` to `a_y_max` in steps. To keep cycle count < 1024, we scale down the iteration steps. Alternatively, we can check the corners and center of the axis-aligned square against the rotated square's condition.
    *   **Condition for point (x, y) in Rotated Square:**
        *   Let `u = x_scaled + y_scaled`
        *   Let `v = x_scaled - y_scaled`
        *   Condition: `b_u_min <= u <= b_u_max` AND `b_v_min <= v <= b_v_max`

3.  **Simplified HDL Logic:**
    *   Check 5 specific points of the Axis-Aligned Square: 4 corners and 1 center point.
    *   If any point satisfies the Rotated Square condition, intersection exists.
    *   This is a heuristic approximation valid for the given constraints (squares are convex).

**Module Interface:**
- **Inputs:**
    - `clk`, `rst_n`: Standard synchronous control.
    - `start`: 1-cycle pulse to initiate calculation.
    - `sq_a_x[0:3]`, `sq_a_y[0:3]`: 4 vertices of axis-aligned square (9-bit signed, 2's complement). Range -100 to 100.
    - `sq_b_x[0:3]`, `sq_b_y[0:3]`: 4 vertices of rotated square (9-bit signed, 2's complement).
- **Outputs:**
    - `result`: 1-bit high if intersection detected.
    - `done`: 1-cycle pulse indicating valid result.

**Implementation Details:**
- Use a Finite State Machine (FSM) with states: IDLE, CALC_EXTREMES, CHECK_POINTS, FINISH.
- In `CHECK_POINTS` state, evaluate the condition for 5 points sequentially (15 cycles total).
- The condition check involves adding/subtracting scaled coordinates and comparing with the pre-calculated min/max bounds of the rotated square.
- Output `result` should be held high if intersection found.
