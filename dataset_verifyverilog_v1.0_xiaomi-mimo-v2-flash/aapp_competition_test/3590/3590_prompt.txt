You must implement a Verilog module that computes the minimum cut cost to extract a smaller convex polygon B from a larger convex polygon A. 

**Simplification Constraints:**
1. **Scale**: Coordinates are integers in range [-1000, 1000] (scaled down from 10^6). Use Q16.16 fixed-point for internal calculations.
2. **Size**: Maximum vertices for A and B is 8 (reduced from 200).
3. **Algorithm**: 
   - Input polygon A and B are given as vertex arrays.
   - The solution involves finding the minimum perimeter of a cut sequence. This reduces to finding the minimum length of a spanning 'cut' path around B.
   - Implement a simplified version: Calculate the distance between vertices of B and A, then find the minimum perimeter that encloses B using the vertices of A as support.
   - Specifically, the cost is the perimeter of the convex hull of the union of A and B? No, that's incorrect. 
   - Correct Logic: The problem asks for the minimum total length of cuts. Each cut is a line segment incident to an edge of B, passing through A.
   - Let's simplify: We will compute the "Shortest Path" wrapping around B. This is often the perimeter of the convex hull of B projected to A's boundary.
   - **Proposed Algorithm**: 
     1. Read A (up to 8 vertices) and B (up to 8 vertices).
     2. Scale inputs to Q16.16 fixed point.
     3. For each edge of B, find the corresponding supporting line tangent to A.
     4. Sum the lengths of these tangents.
     5. Use dynamic programming: `dp[i][j]` represents the minimum cost to cover the perimeter up to vertex `j` of B using vertex `i` of A.

**Module Interface:**
- Inputs: 
  - `clk`, `rst_n`: Standard clock and active-low reset.
  - `start`: 1-cycle pulse to begin computation.
  - `a_valid`, `b_valid`: Flags indicating vertex data availability.
  - `a_x`, `a_y`: 32-bit signed (16.16 fixed point) coordinates for vertex of A.
  - `b_x`, `b_y`: 32-bit signed coordinates for vertex of B.
- Outputs:
  - `result`: 64-bit fixed-point result (32.32 for precision) representing the minimum cost.
  - `done`: 1-cycle pulse when result is ready.
  - `busy`: High while computing.

**Implementation Steps:**
1. **Storage**: Store A and B in 8-slot arrays (packed or unpacked). 
2. **Fixed-Point Math**: Implement `add`, `sub`, `mul` modules for 32-bit Q16.16.
3. **Distance**: Euclidean distance `sqrt(dx^2 + dy^2)`. Approximate sqrt using fixed-point iterations (e.g., 8 iterations of Newton-Raphson).
4. **DP Logic**:
   - Iterate through edges of B (0 to N-1).
   - For each edge, iterate through vertices of A (0 to M-1) to find the "tangent" point that minimizes the cut length.
   - Accumulate cost.
5. **Control**: 
   - `LOAD`: Wait for `start`, load vertices into arrays.
   - `CALC`: Perform geometry checks (cross products) to determine tangency and sum lengths.
   - `DONE`: Assert done signal.
